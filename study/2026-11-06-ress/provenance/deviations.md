# Deviations from the registered plan

1. **Tie handling in AP, AUROC, ECE and top-decile gap (found after viewing held-out results).** `event_study.metrics` and `probability_metrics` broke tied scores by row order. State-only and tree models produce many ties, so the rank metrics partly encoded time order. Example symptom: AUROC near 0.00 for M1. `extensions/rescore.py` recomputes these from the saved scores with tied groups. No model, score, threshold, calibrator, alarm or SHAP value changed. The original values remain in `runs/event-study/run.json`; only `tables/primary-metrics.csv` should be cited.
2. **Calibration slope after Platt.** Where Platt's slope is 0, every test prediction in that fold is the same probability, so a post-calibration slope is undefined. Values in `run.json` for those cells are numerical artifacts and are reported as undefined.
3. **Exploratory threshold check (added after viewing results).** Because the registered policy caught nothing, `tables/exploratory-oracle-threshold.csv` takes thresholds from each test block's own negatives. This uses test information and is an optimistic upper bound. It is not part of any rule.
4. **Registered settings not done.** No hyperparameter tuning was run, as registered. Analysis A M4 and analysis B to E use one seed. M3 SHAP uses 5 seeds in analysis A only.
5. **Random-split comparison (exploratory, added after viewing results).** `extensions/leakage_demo.py` fits M3 on a random 60% of decisions, which lets rows from the same failure appear on both sides. It is included only to show how much that split alone inflates scores. It is not an estimate of warning ability.
6. **Single-feature direction check (exploratory, added after viewing results).** `extensions/sanity.py` computes block-level AUROC for load share and oil temperature without any model. It is descriptive, unadjusted for season and duty, and does not establish a mechanism.
7. **Sequence-model rule table (added with the addendum).** `extensions/seq_tables.py` applies the first five registered rules to M5 and M6, reading "M3 above M1" as "the model above M1", as the addendum's primary comparison requires. Post-calibration slope is counted as out of range where undefined.
8. **Rank-score chance control (exploratory, added after viewing M5 and M6 results).** `extensions/rank_chance.py` rotates labels around each held-out sequence by 1,000 offsets and recomputes AUROC with scores fixed. It uses saved scores only and reproduces the frozen AUROC values to 4e-7, the rounding of saved margins. A first version counted censored decisions (label -1) as positives; the check against frozen tables caught it, and no result from that version was used. The shares are descriptive and unadjusted across cells.
9. **Figure and text rounding.** Recomputing claims from the tables found that M3's lowest median AUROC is 0.1550 (0.15, not the 0.16 in the EAAI draft) and M6's highest median false-alarm rate is 1.5747 (1.57). The RESS manuscript uses the recomputed values. The EAAI draft in `submission/` is kept as it was.
10. **Larger-model phases dropped (addendum 2, section 4).** On 2026-11-07, while the run was in progress and before any larger model had been fitted or scored, the author decided to drop the `larger` and `larger-heldin` phases to save 3 to 4.5 hours. The reason was run time, together with the training-fit check showing median training AUROC of 0.96 to 1.00 for the small models. M5 and M6 therefore remain small, and the manuscript must keep that limitation.
11. **Alarm timing and onset profile (exploratory, added after viewing the label-timing results).** `extensions/gaps_onset.py` records when alarms fired relative to the reported start under the +60 shift and how sensors behave around each start. It fits nothing. It was not in addendum 2. A first reading counted all 32 alarms as after the start; the short-horizon catch windows lie at or after the start by construction, so the manuscript rests this point on the 16 alarms at horizons of 3 hours or more.
12. **Addendum 2 tables written after the run.** `extensions/gaps_tables.py` was written once the runs finished. It asserts that its rule code reproduces `tables/seq-rule-outcomes.csv` before use. The addendum fixes the rules and comparisons; this script only computes them. Its reading of "slope in range" requires Platt b above 0, as item 2 and item 7 do.
13. **Further-interval check (exploratory, added after reading Toribio et al. in full).** `extensions/extra_intervals_check.py` compares the twelve further intervals in their Table 1 with sustained loaded runs. It fits nothing and changes no registered result. Its counts of runs are now in the manuscript as part of addendum 3.
14. **Addendum 3 code fix before any output.** The first run of `extensions/extra16.py` stopped on the exploratory random-split entry in `runs/seq-study/run.json`, which is not a registered fold and has no score file. No table had been written. The fold filter was added and the hashes in `provenance/addendum-intervals-freeze-hashes.txt` were recomputed, so those hashes postdate that fix. `extensions/extra16_tables.py` was written after the addendum, and it reported rule outcomes when first run; it applies the addendum's stated limits. M2 and M4 have no analysis E, so their burden rule is not defined, but they caught no interval and cannot reach the warning share.
15. **Load share before reports (exploratory, added after reading Kulkarni and Digalwar and Alhammadi et al.).** `extensions/onset_load_share.py` compares load share in the six hours before each report with report-free windows of the same length. It fits nothing. Windows overlap, so the 3.9% is descriptive. It led to a caveat on the Discussion sentence about reporting lag.
16. **Alarm definitions (addendum 4, after an outside review).** The registered code credits a catch from the unmerged signal and counts false alarms from merged notifications over scored minutes. `extensions/alarm_defs.py` recounts from saved scores. New alerts: registered 0 of 250, seeds 3 of 280, +60 from 32 to 29, further intervals 11 of 11. Decision days are 96.7% of scored days; no burden outcome changes. Two of 860 cells differ by one false alarm (M3, analysis D, -60, 30 and 180 min) because tied scores equal to the threshold were rounded above it when saved. The registered definitions stay primary.
17. **Text changes from the same review.** The onset sentence no longer says sensors support reported starts to within 25 minutes: load is an operating state. Zero Platt coefficients were called slopes in one Results sentence, and this is corrected. The study records no rationale for the 5-minute and 1-per-day limits beyond inspection taking time to arrange, so the manuscript calls them study rules and describes what they mean, not why they are right.
18. **Boundary of the catch window (found after a second outside review).** The registered plan says a lead of at least 5 minutes; `event_policy` excludes the minute exactly 5 minutes before the start, so the applied rule is more than 5 minutes. `extensions/alarm_boundary.py` shows that including it changes nothing in the registered or timing analyses and adds three further-seed catches (M6, 5 June), from 3 to 6 of 280. No rule outcome changes. Frozen results stay as computed and the difference is disclosed. The twelve further intervals were not rechecked for this boundary.
19. **Label interval description (third outside review).** Methods gave the positive interval as $[t, t+h)$; the code labels the $h$ minutes before onset, which is $(t, t+h]$. The text is corrected. No saved result changes.
20. **Load share before reports corrected (final read-through).** `extensions/onset_load_share.py` first counted minutes with COMP below 0.5, which gave 1% before 18 April because the compressor cycles within minutes, and disagreed with Figure 3 and Table 7. It now uses the mean of 1-COMP, as Figure 3 does: 50% before 15 July, 20%, 15% and 14% before the others, against a median of 11%, with 3.9% of report-free windows at or above the 15 July level. Sustained-run counts still use COMP below 0.5 by design. Table 7's values differ slightly because they average a 60-minute lookback.
